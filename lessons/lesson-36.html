Time manipulation round 2
2026.09.30

I realized my article yesterday was really sloppy, especially the part about the baseball and the flashlight. This is what happens when I want to sound cool but have basically zero understanding of the theory of relativity. Let me try again today and then it's back to a brief but fun use case of time manipulation.

I'll still stick with trains to set the scene. Suppose we have station A and B and both are 1.8 million km apart. There is a clock in each station and the staff at the platform synced the clocks beforehand and the passengers in the train also synced their watches. Let's say it's 10:00:00 when the train passes station A. Let the train travel at 60% of the speed of light, so 180,000km/s. The train then passes station B. The clock at station B reads 10:00:10 as the train passes, but the passenger's watch reads 10:00:08. What?

To understand this, we need to imagine a sprite (like the fairy) oscillating. Let's say there are two sprites. There's one sprite on the platform and one on the train. Imagine the sprite is a messenger and has to constantly bring messages from a point on the floor to a point directly above in the ceiling. Assume the floor to ceiling distance is 4 steps and identical on the platform and on the train. The sprite isn't allowed to stop and has to travel at the speed of light. The platform sprite just oscillates. The train sprite is also oscillating but also moves forward due to the train. From the perspective of a platform observer, the sprite is no longer ONLY taking 4 steps upwards but is also traveling diagonally due to the forward movement of the train. (Of course for a passenger on the train, the sprite is still taking 4 steps upwards.) For simplicity, say the train moves the sprite 3 steps forward, so using Pythagoras theorem, the train sprite takes 5 steps diagonally to reach the ceiling. To the platform observer, the 5 steps needed by the train sprite take longer than the 4 steps needed by the platform sprite since the speed is constant. In other words, the platform observer perceives the train sprite as slower than the platform sprite. The faster the train the longer the diagonal. We can interpret the sprite as a really simple timekeeper. Each oscillation (floor to ceiling OR ceiling to floor) is a tick. So when the train reached point B, the platform clock would have ticked 10 times, but the train clock only 8. The faster the train, the more steps it takes to complete one tick.

I kept emphasizing "from the perspective of the platform observer". We can also look at the perspective of a passenger on the train. The sprite is oscillating but not moving forward since both are in the train. In fact, the platform seems to be zipping past backwards at 60% of light speed. So, just reverse the situation above. To a passenger on a train, the platform sprite is actually moving backwards diagonally, as a result is perceived to tick slower than the train sprite. While the train keeps moving at a steady speed, neither view is more correct than the other. It's all relative. THAT'S why it's called the theory of relativity! The only way to settle whether the platform observer or passenger actually aged less is to stop the train and all the clocks and then compare them!

If you are starting to experience baldness, time manipulation can really help. You can carefully smash raw time together so that there is one universe where you have hair, isolate only that event and you in the point of time where you have hair so as to not engage in mass genocide just for hair. Then freeze that point and fold it into your current timeline and knit that point together with all past and future points so that you never lose hair. Maybe, you don't even need to mess with raw time. If there is an existing universe where you don't lose your hair, you might be able to duplicate, freeze and then knit that point so that you have hair in the current universe. I think it might be a bit like Orihime's Souten Kisshun ability.
