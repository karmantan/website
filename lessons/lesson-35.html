Lesson #35
Manipulating time is more than tinkering with the flow of time
2026.09.29

When people think about time manipulation, they are likely picturing time travel, time loops, time reversal and the trickiest one for me to grasp, time dilation. All of these deal with the flow of time. If time is a straight line, time travel is jumping from one point to another, a time loop is traveling from point A to point B and then jumping back from B to A repeatedly, time reversal is jumping back to a previous point in the flow and then continuing. For time dilation, let's first start with an example.

Since the speed of light is a universal constant, (or in other words the maximum speed) in regular day-to-day situations, we would not perceive any difference when light travels. Suppose I'm in a train traveling at 300km/h and I throw a baseball and it travels at a speed of 10km/h. Because I am "stationary" in the train, I perceive the baseball's speed to be 10km/h. To a person on the platform, the train is zipping past. I am already not stationary since I am traveling at 300km/h. The baseball is additionally travelling at 10km/h. To the person on the platform, the baseball is actually covering extra distance, so approximately 310 kilometers per hour.

Now, instead of a baseball, say I am shining a flashlight upwards for simplicity. The light travels at the speed of light to me and it also goes straight up. To the person on the platform, the light is actually tracing a diagonal since the train is moving, so it travels a longer distance. With the baseball, the person on the platform perceives the extra distance as more speed. However, since the speed of light is constant, speed can't be used to explain the extra distance as it's 300,000km/s for both of us. The only variable which remains is time. The light covers a longer distance at the same speed from the platform, which means that the denominator, time also has to be larger. So say it's 10:00:00. Let's assume for simplicity, it takes 1 second for the light to reach the ceiling. For me, the clock would read 10:00:01. On the platform, it takes longer, maybe 5 seconds. Their clock would read 10:00:05. Realistically, at a regular train speed of 300km/h, the difference between the clocks would be super tiny and nowhere near 4 seconds. The train would need to be going at near light speed for the ratio to be 1:5.

If we want to revert to the time as a line analogy, let time be on the x-axis and space (distance) be on the y-axis. Let the speed of light be a concave frontier since everything moves through space-time with a combined speed that always equals the speed of light. Normally, we are all experiencing the flow of time but traveling way way way below light speed, i.e. we are on the frontier close to the x-axis, so our clock is normal. If we travel faster, we move upwards along the frontier, so the share of the "budget" going to time shrinks so our clock runs slower than normal. This is time dilation.

All of the above is still related to the flow of time and still what most people mean when they talk about time manipulation in sci-fi. But, I would imagine true time manipulation to be a larger feat than that. In Loki, the flow of time is often referred to as manifested time. Manifested time is woven or processed raw time. In Loki, it seems that the randomness observed in manifested timelines (or alternate universes) isn't actually that random but tightly controlled, in the sense that the TVA will delete a timeline that wasn't authorized by He Who Remains. So, a single choice might have a non-zero probability of three different outcomes (which spawn maybe three different timelines) but that's about it. Of course, following the TVA reform, a single choice can have a non-zero probability of infinite outcomes, so infinite timelines or universes are spawned. A more skilled time manipulator might be able to smash two timelines together, and all the choices contained in that timeline probably interact or cannibalize each other, if the timelines are smashed together "chronologically". But, it could also be the case that timelines are smashed together so that the beginning of one timeline is touching the end of another timeline. Or timelines are folded, so the past, present and future are occurring simultaneously. So a time manipulator can create more chaos or let the chaos cannibalize itself. I think.

Raw time is really difficult to imagine. I feel like it's still related to probabilities but maybe a better interpretation is that it's a chaotic soup, where everything and nothing are all happening at once? I am not even sure if reality (as we know it) exists in raw time. Like probably, the building blocks are there. The building blocks are probably potential energy (in the physical and emotional sense lol). Maybe a nice interpretation is like the primordial chaos in Bleach that Yhwach wants to return to. It's just a soup, and sometimes things in the soup coagulate to form consciousness but it can easily collapse too. I wonder what happens if a time manipulator takes a ball of raw time and smashes it into a timeline. I'm guessing in that universe, time would unfold and someone can see past or future versions of themselves, or be everything all at once. Everything would be unpredictable and probably fleeting.

I've been thinking of the time manipulator as somewhat destructive. But of course, they can be benevolent. If two balls of raw time were smashed together, the friction alone might be able to create very unstable timelines. If let's say someone needs a cure for cancer, one timeline could potentially arise where a cure for cancer is developed, but the timeline keeps unraveling because friction alone might not be enough to sustain it. The time manipulator can weave together the timelines much like Loki did to ensure that the timeline survives. Let's say these two specific balls of raw time weren't able to generate the cure for cancer. It might be possible to unravel and restitch the timelines from the same balls of raw time (OK, this isn't very benevolent). Or maybe, take two other balls and try again. Glorious purpose indeed.
