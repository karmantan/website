Lesson #34
It's impossible to say mundane things as a celebrity
2026.09.05

Hayao Miyazaki is famously gloomy, and because his movies are beloved by many people, his words have often been considered profound. I don't think his claims about doom and gloom are unique. "Humans have both the urge to create and destroy", "The future is clear. It's going to fall apart", "Everything is so thin and shallow and fake", etc. Plenty of people say those words. Depending on who says it, there's a 50-50 chance it gets ignored or people eat it up and then forget it in 2 business days. When Miyazaki says it though, people treat those phrases like previously unknown revelations about the world. When sharing his "wisdom", they justify the profundity by saying he's a pacifist, anti-capitalist and environmentalist.

Not only that, but because people feel connected to Studio Ghibli, they want Miyazaki to sort of be an oracle and also misattribute positions he doesn't hold to elevate his status. Some people have called Miyazaki a Marxist, whereas he has given up on that and stated a preference for small communities (which I am interpreting as distributism and anti-trust?). Furthermore, Miyazaki is called an environmentalist because of Princess Mononoke, while he himself says the film is about the complex relationship between people and nature. Not only are his positions not unique, but the labels are also off.

Another example is Eileen Gu. She essentially said a fancier version of "I think, therefore, I am" or "I'm locked in" and people. eat. it. up. It's obvious to everyone, in particular, those who have been to therapy. Her answer to the reporter can essentially be summarized as introspection and discipline. But she used the word "scientist", so I guess people wanted to relate because scientists are often portrayed positively. Who wouldn't want to fancy themselves scientists? That's probably why her answer is considered more intelligent than other athletes who say the same thing but with fewer words. It's not fair to the other athletes, I think.
