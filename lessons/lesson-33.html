Lesson #33
To understand the quantum realm, think of fabric
2026.09.05

In Ant Man, I always get confused as to how shrinking past subatomic level allows one to enter into another dimension. Not just another dimension in terms of the microscopic world, but into a parallel universe where space-time doesn't hold in the sense that we know.

This might not be accurate, but an interpretation I came up with yesterday. Universes are bound by fabric, and maybe we shouldn't think of the position of universes as next to each other on a 2/3/4D plane. Try thinking of universes are superimposed on each other and the boundary of each universe has its own fabric. It is important to note that universes may share the fabric boundary at some points and not at others. This fabric, for slightly less than 100% of the time, keeps each universe from interacting with each other. All fabrics have a threadcount, right? We can think of the universe boundary fabric as having an exceptionally high threadcount.

Regardless of threadcount, fabric is still porous (I guess). So, if you are able to shrink to the point where you can easily pass through the fabric, then you might be able to enter another universe. Furthermore, recall before that we should note that universes do not always share fabric boundaries. This means that there must be a gap between fabric boundaries and universes. This gap is where the quantum realm exists.

If one shrinks to a subatomic level and enters the gap instead of another universe, then that person is in the quantum realm. What is still unclear to me is whether the individual maintains their shrunken size or not. There is no reason to assume that the individual cannot exist at normal size in the quantum realm, but just needs to shrink in order to reach it. Once you reach and arrive at the shrunken size, you can probably resize unless there are other quantum effects that I ignored.
